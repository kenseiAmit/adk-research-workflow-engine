## Research Findings

Leonard Susskind, a leading theoretical physicist, approaches the concept of free will not through direct philosophical treatises but by framing it within the fundamental laws of physics, primarily quantum mechanics and general relativity. He consistently emphasizes a universe governed by deterministic or probabilistically deterministic principles, as evidenced by the Schrödinger equation's description of wave function evolution and the information preservation tenets of black hole physics and the holographic principle. Susskind's work, particularly his 'Theoretical Minimum' series, underscores a rule-bound cosmos where physical processes unfold according to strict, albeit complex, laws, leaving little room for a libertarian, acausal free will.

Susskind generally espouses a compatibilist viewpoint, suggesting that our subjective experience of free will can be reconciled with a physically determined reality. He interprets free will as an emergent phenomenon or a 'useful illusion' that arises from the intricate complexity of the brain and consciousness. Rather than a fundamental property that violates physical laws, he views our sense of making choices as a high-level description of underlying physical and electrochemical processes. This perspective is frequently highlighted in his informal discussions and Q&A sessions following his lectures, where he often challenges the notion of a free will that exists outside the framework of physical laws.

While Susskind does not explicitly declare a universe devoid of free will, his discourse invites a re-evaluation of its meaning under a scientific, physicalist worldview. He advocates for understanding consciousness and decision-making as phenomena fully integrated within the physical universe, rather than as external or non-physical influences. His arguments implicitly challenge interpretations of quantum indeterminacy as a loophole for free will, consistently grounding complex phenomena as consequences of fundamental physical laws.
